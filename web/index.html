Save your schedule from DraftMySchedule

Slotted needs one file: your DraftMySchedule page, saved from your browser. It takes about a minute: four quick steps. Tick each one off as you go.

  1. Sign in with your Western account. Use a laptop or desktop: phones can't easily save a whole web page.

  2. After you sign in, DraftMySchedule opens on Build Future Draft. That page is for planning drafts, not the classes you're registered in, so switch to My Current Schedule.

    HomeCourse ListBuild DraftAccess Saved DraftsMy Shared DraftsMy Current Schedule← click this oneEnrollment Appointment

  3. Stay on My Current Schedule; there's no need to scroll or click anything else. Saving keeps the whole page, including the class list Slotted reads.

    Press Ctrl + S, set the format to Webpage, HTML Only, then click Save.

    Steps for other browsers
    • Chrome or Edge: Ctrl + S (⌘ + S on a Mac), set the format to Webpage, HTML Only, then Save.
    • Safari: choose File → Save As…, set Format to Page Source, then Save.
    • Firefox: Ctrl + S (⌘ + S on a Mac), choose Web Page, HTML only, then Save.

    Save the page itself, not DraftMySchedule's PDF. The PDF doesn't keep the rooms and times in a form Slotted can read.

  4. It's usually in your Downloads folder, named The University of Western Ontario.html.

      Add it to your calendar

      1. Open the file. On a Mac, double-click it in Downloads. On an iPhone or iPad, tap it in the Files app or in your downloads.
      2. Choose where the classes go. On a Mac, pick New Calendar so your classes have their own calendar you can hide, recolour or remove in one go. On iPhone or iPad, tap Add All.
      3. That's it. With iCloud turned on, your classes appear on your other Apple devices automatically.
      1. On a computer, go to calendar.google.com. Google only imports files on a computer; afterwards your classes show up on your phone too.
      2. Make a calendar just for classes. Go to Settings → Add calendar → Create new calendar and name it something like “Western Classes”. Google can't undo an import, so a separate calendar lets you delete all the classes in one step if you ever need to.
      3. Import the file. Go to Settings → Import & export, select the downloaded file, choose your new calendar, then click Import.
      4. Turn on reminders (optional). Open your new calendar's settings and add an Event notification, for example 10 minutes before.
      1. Outlook on the web: open Calendar, choose Add calendar → Upload from file, select the downloaded file, choose a calendar, then click Import.
      2. Outlook on Windows or Mac: double-click the downloaded file and Outlook will offer to import the events. Choose a calendar for them.
      3. The reminder you picked is included and works in Outlook.
      1. Open the file with your calendar app, or look for an Import option in its settings or menu.
      2. If it asks where to put the events, choose a new, separate calendar, so your classes can be hidden or removed together later.
      3. The file uses the standard iCalendar (.ics) format, which almost every calendar app supports.

      Something not right? Compare a few classes with DraftMySchedule. If your schedule changes later, come back and make a new file.
